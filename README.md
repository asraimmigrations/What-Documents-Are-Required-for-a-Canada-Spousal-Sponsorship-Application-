# What Documents Are Required for a Canada Spousal Sponsorship Application

## Introduction

Building a life together in Canada is an important goal for many couples. Whether one partner is already living in Canada or the other is applying from abroad, spousal sponsorship can provide a pathway to Canadian permanent residence. However, preparing the application requires more than completing a few forms. Couples must provide accurate personal information, legal documents, and convincing evidence that their relationship is genuine.

For French-speaking couples in Quebec and across Canada, understanding the documentation requirements early can make the application process easier to manage. Some documents are straightforward to obtain, while others may require time, especially when they come from another country or need to be translated into English or French.

This guide explains the main documents commonly required for a Canadian spousal sponsorship application, how to organize them, and what applicants should consider before submitting their files. **Asra Immigration**, available at [asraimmigration.ca](https://asraimmigration.ca/), can be a starting point for people seeking information and guidance about Canadian immigration pathways.

Immigration requirements depend on each couple’s circumstances. Always follow the latest instructions and personalized document checklist issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC).

## 1. A Valid Marriage Certificate

For married couples, a valid marriage certificate is one of the most important documents in a spousal sponsorship application. It provides official evidence that the marriage was legally registered in the jurisdiction where it took place.

The certificate should clearly identify both spouses and show the legally registered marriage. If the marriage occurred outside Canada, applicants should check the country-specific instructions to confirm which civil document is accepted.

A marriage licence or a record showing that a ceremony took place may not be sufficient. IRCC generally requires proof that the marriage was legally registered with the relevant government authority.

If the certificate is unavailable, delayed, or contains incorrect information, contact the issuing authority to explore the available options. Do not substitute an unrelated document without checking the official requirements.

It is also important to understand that a marriage certificate alone does not establish every aspect of a genuine relationship. Applicants should be prepared to provide additional evidence showing the history and development of their relationship.

## 2. Passports and Identity Documents

Both the sponsor and the person being sponsored may need to provide identity and civil-status documents. The exact requirements depend on the application checklist, country of residence, nationality, and individual circumstances.

The sponsored spouse will generally need a copy of the relevant passport pages, including pages that show personal details, the passport number, issue date, expiry date, and other information requested by IRCC.

Additional identity documents may include:

- Birth certificates.
- National identity cards, where required.
- Previous passports or travel documents, if requested.
- Documents showing a legal name change.
- Other civil-status records specified in the country-specific checklist.

The sponsor may also need to provide evidence of their Canadian status. Depending on their situation, this could include a Canadian citizenship certificate, Canadian passport, permanent resident card, or another accepted document confirming their status.

Make sure names and dates are consistent across all records. If a name differs because of marriage, transliteration, a spelling variation, or a legal change, include the appropriate supporting evidence and explanation.

## 3. Proof That the Relationship Is Genuine

One of the most important parts of a spousal sponsorship application is evidence that the relationship is genuine and not entered into primarily for immigration purposes.

IRCC may review the couple’s relationship history, living arrangements, shared responsibilities, communication, and other details. The goal is not to collect the largest possible number of documents. It is to present relevant, credible evidence that gives a clear picture of the relationship.

Depending on the couple’s circumstances and the applicable checklist, useful evidence may include:

**Photographs together:** Photos from different stages of the relationship can help demonstrate shared experiences. Include context where useful, such as the date, location, or event.

**Communication records:** Selected messages, emails, call logs, or other communication records can help show how the relationship developed, particularly when the partners have lived apart.

**Travel records:** Flight itineraries, boarding passes, hotel reservations, and travel stamps may help establish visits and time spent together.

**Evidence of shared finances:** Joint bank accounts, shared expenses, insurance documents, or other financial records may demonstrate how the couple manages responsibilities together.

**Evidence of a shared home:** Joint leases, property documents, utility bills, or official correspondence showing the same address can support a claim that the couple lives together.

**Evidence of public recognition:** Documents showing that the relationship is recognized by family, friends, employers, or institutions may be useful in the circumstances.

**Letters or statements from people who know the couple:** These may provide additional context if relevant to the application requirements.

Applicants should choose evidence that reflects their actual circumstances. A couple who has lived together for several years may have different documentation from a couple who has maintained a long-distance relationship because of work, studies, or immigration restrictions.

Do not manufacture evidence or change records to make the relationship appear different from reality. Inconsistencies and misleading information can seriously affect an immigration application.

## 4. Documents for Common-Law Partners

Not every couple applying for sponsorship is legally married. Canadian immigration rules also recognize eligible common-law partnerships.

Generally, common-law partners must have lived together in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 consecutive months to meet the basic cohabitation requirement. Applicants must still meet the other applicable eligibility criteria.

Evidence may include:

- Joint rental agreements or property ownership records.
- Utility bills and official correspondence showing the shared address.
- Joint financial or insurance records.
- Government documents showing the same residential address.
- Other relevant records demonstrating continuous cohabitation.

If the couple has not been able to obtain conventional joint documents, they should carefully review the official checklist and determine what alternative evidence may be accepted. A clear explanation can help provide context, but it does not automatically replace a required document.

Couples should not assume that living together for a short period or being in a committed relationship automatically qualifies them as common-law partners.

## 5. Immigration Forms and Application Checklist

Spousal sponsorship involves both a sponsorship application and an application for permanent residence. The required forms depend on the type of relationship, the family members included, and the applicants’ individual circumstances.

Common forms for spouse and partner sponsorship include the following:

**IMM 1344 — Application to Sponsor, Sponsorship Agreement and Undertaking.** This form concerns the sponsorship commitment and must be completed and signed by the required parties.

**IMM 5532 — Relationship Information and Sponsorship Evaluation.** This form gathers information about the sponsor, the applicant, and the relationship.

**IMM 0008 — Generic Application Form for Canada.** The principal applicant completes this permanent residence application form.

**IMM 5669 — Schedule A: Background/Declaration.** This form collects background information and personal history. Required family members may also need to complete it.

**IMM 5406 — Additional Family Information.** This form collects information about family relationships.

**IMM 5562 — Supplementary Information: Your Travels.** This form is required when applicable under the current instructions.

**IMM 5476 — Use of a Representative.** This form is relevant when an applicant or sponsor formally appoints a representative, where required.

This is a general overview, not a complete list for every applicant. The official application package may require other forms or documents. Use the current checklist for the exact application category rather than relying on an old checklist or a list copied from another applicant.

The sponsor and applicant should also confirm that all required signatures and declarations have been completed correctly. Missing forms, incomplete answers, or incorrect signatures can lead to an application being returned or delayed.

## 6. Birth Certificates and Documents for Dependent Children

If dependent children are included in the application, additional documentation may be required. Depending on the circumstances, this can include birth certificates, identity documents, custody records, adoption documents, or other evidence of the parent-child relationship.

Applicants must declare family members as required by IRCC, including family members who are not accompanying them to Canada. Failing to declare a family member or comply with required examination rules can have serious consequences for future immigration applications.

Where only one parent is travelling with a child, additional consent or custody documentation may be needed, depending on the situation and applicable checklist.

Check the requirements for each child individually. A document that is sufficient for one family member may not satisfy the requirements for another.

## 7. Police Certificates and Background Information

Police certificates may be required as part of the permanent residence process. The specific requirements depend on the applicant’s age, residence history, and IRCC instructions.

Applicants should not assume that they need a certificate from every country they have ever visited. Instead, review the official instructions to determine which countries and periods of residence are relevant.

Police certificates can take time to obtain, particularly when they must be requested from another country. Begin researching the process early and follow the issuing authority’s instructions carefully.

If a certificate is unavailable, delayed, or difficult to obtain, review IRCC’s guidance on what to do. Provide an explanation and any supporting evidence requested by the applicable instructions. An explanation does not guarantee that IRCC will accept the missing document.

Applicants should also ensure that their addresses, travel history, employment history, and other background information are accurate and consistent across the application forms.

## 8. Medical Examination and Biometrics

A medical examination and biometrics are important parts of many permanent residence applications, but they are not necessarily documents that every applicant must obtain before submitting the initial application.

IRCC will provide instructions about the medical examination when applicable. Applicants should follow the timing and process specified for their case rather than booking an examination based on outdated advice.

Biometrics usually involve fingerprints and a photograph. When required, the applicant receives instructions explaining how and where to provide them.

Depending on the case, applicants may also need to pay applicable biometrics fees and attend an appointment within the deadline stated in the instruction letter.

Keep copies of appointment confirmations, receipts, and relevant correspondence. Respond promptly to IRCC requests and monitor the online account for updates.

## 9. Certified Translations of Documents

Many applicants submit documents issued in countries where the official records are not in English or French. For couples in Quebec and French-speaking communities, it is especially important to distinguish between documents already issued in French and documents that require translation.

If a document is in neither English nor French, IRCC generally requires a translation and supporting documentation in accordance with its current rules. Depending on the circumstances, this may include a certified copy of the original document and an affidavit from the translator.

Applicants should check the exact translation instructions before paying for translation services. Requirements can differ depending on the document and the person who translated it.

Relationship evidence such as letters, messages, emails, or other communication records may also need translation if it is not already in English or French. IRCC’s guide sets limits and specific conditions for certain supporting materials, so applicants should follow the applicable checklist rather than uploading an excessive volume of unrequested records.

Keep the original documents and clear copies. IRCC may ask to examine an original or request additional evidence during processing.

## 10. Country-Specific Civil Documents

The documents required for an application can vary according to where the sponsored person lives and where their civil records were issued.

For example, different countries may have different procedures for issuing marriage certificates, birth certificates, police certificates, national identity documents, or records of name changes.

Some applicants may need additional civil-status records or explanations because of local administrative practices. Others may need to show why a particular document cannot be obtained.

Before submitting, use IRCC’s official application tool to select the country where the sponsored person currently lives and the country or countries where the relevant documents will be issued. Follow the instructions for those countries carefully.

This step is especially important for international couples who have lived in several countries or whose marriage and birth records were issued in different jurisdictions.

## 11. Additional Documents for Applicants Living in Quebec

Quebec has additional provincial procedures for certain family sponsorship cases. A federal spousal sponsorship application and Quebec’s undertaking process are related, but they are not identical.

Depending on the case, the sponsor may need to complete a provincial undertaking process in addition to the federal requirements. The timing and eligibility rules for Quebec can change, so applicants should review the current provincial instructions before submitting provincial documents.

Do not send an undertaking application to Quebec prematurely. Follow IRCC’s instructions about when to begin the provincial stage and consult the relevant Quebec government guidance for current procedures.

For French-speaking couples living in Montreal, Quebec City, Laval, Gatineau, or elsewhere in the province, understanding both federal and provincial requirements can help prevent avoidable errors.

## 12. Proof of the Sponsor’s Status and Eligibility

The sponsor must demonstrate that they meet the eligibility rules for sponsoring a spouse or partner. Depending on their situation, they may need to provide proof of Canadian citizenship, permanent resident status, or another eligible status under the applicable rules.

Other details can affect eligibility, including the sponsor’s place of residence, age, previous sponsorship undertakings, and certain financial or legal circumstances.

There is generally no minimum income requirement for sponsoring a spouse or partner in many standard cases, but exceptions and other obligations can apply, particularly when dependent children with dependent children are involved. Sponsors also undertake financial responsibilities for the sponsored person for the applicable period.

Applicants should review the current eligibility requirements rather than assuming that proof of income is always required or never required.

## 13. How to Organize Your Spousal Sponsorship Documents

A well-organized application makes it easier to check that each requirement has been addressed. Before uploading documents, create a folder for identity records, relationship evidence, forms, civil-status documents, translations, and any documents relating to dependent children.

Use clear filenames that identify the document and the person it belongs to. Make sure scans are readable, complete, and saved in the file format and size accepted by the online portal.

Review every question on the forms and compare the answers with the supporting records. Dates, addresses, relationship history, travel history, and family details should be consistent. If something genuinely differs between documents, explain the reason rather than ignoring the discrepancy.

Finally, check the application against the current personalized checklist. IRCC warns that incomplete applications can be returned without processing. If a requested document cannot be provided, follow the official instructions and include a detailed explanation and supporting evidence where appropriate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most important document for spousal sponsorship in Canada?

For a married couple, a valid marriage certificate is a key document. However, the application also requires the correct forms, identity records, and evidence of a genuine relationship. No single document guarantees approval.

### Do we need to submit proof of our relationship if we have a marriage certificate?

Yes. A marriage certificate establishes the legal marriage, but IRCC may also require evidence demonstrating that the relationship is genuine. The type and amount of evidence depend on the couple’s circumstances and the current checklist.

### Do documents need to be translated into English or French?

Documents issued in neither English nor French generally require a translation that meets IRCC’s rules. Check the official instructions for certification, affidavits, and supporting copies before arranging translations.

### Can I apply for spousal sponsorship if my spouse lives outside Canada?

Yes, eligible sponsors may generally sponsor a spouse living abroad through the applicable Family Class process. The appropriate application route and document requirements depend on the couple’s circumstances.

### Is the document checklist the same for every country?

No. The general application package is accompanied by instructions that may depend on where the sponsored person lives and where their documents were issued. Always consult the current personalized checklist.

### What happens if I submit an incomplete application?

IRCC may return an incomplete application without processing it. Review all forms and checklist items before submitting. If a required document cannot be provided, follow IRCC’s instructions for explaining the situation.

### Does a spousal sponsorship consultant guarantee approval?

No. A representative may assist with document preparation and application procedures, but no one can guarantee a positive immigration decision. Applicants should verify the credentials of any paid representative.
